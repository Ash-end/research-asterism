# Assumption-led reasoning and mechanism tests

Use when the next decision depends on what a proposed mechanism assumes, what information is missing, whether a principle transfers, or what a failed test actually tested. Select useful moves; do not impose a new questionnaire, document hierarchy, physical reduction, or mathematical formalization on every discipline.

## Decompose before naming a solution

Start with the question and observations, keeping the current explanation separate. Identify the object, target quantity or interpretive object, accessible observations, constraints, and decision. A method's name and a statement about the “essence” of a problem do not establish any of these.

Distinguish definitions and logical consequences from empirical regularities, modeling choices, and untested premises. Mark the basis of each consequential assumption: a supplied source, a derivation, an observed pattern, or a provisional hypothesis. An attractive premise does not become a fact through repeated use.

For information-based tasks, trace observation → representation → readout → decision. A deterministic transformation may expose useful structure to a restricted model without adding new information. Conversely, success by one model does not establish an information-theoretic limit. State the model family, distribution and assumptions before making a capability or impossibility claim.

For qualitative work, clarify which participants, materials and contexts support an interpretation; seek rival readings and discrepant accounts. For theory, state the domain, quantifiers and dependencies, and construct the smallest relevant counterexample. For engineering, specify the delivered function, constraints and failure modes. Use the corresponding [claim-design branch](claim-design.md), rather than forcing all claims into one experiment.

## Generate explanations with different consequences

Consider only distinctions that could change the next decision: unavailable information, omitted structure, a readout unable to use it, an ineffective implementation, or a mismatch between evaluation and the real objective. This is a menu of possible explanations, not an exhaustive taxonomy or a diagnosis from one score.

Contribute a candidate when joint ideation is requested. State what changes, why that change could matter, which premise it needs, the strongest plausible rival, and a prediction on which they disagree. No fixed number of ideas is required. A simple established tool can support a new question; a larger collection of modules does not establish a contribution.

## Transfer a principle by its conditions

An analogy is a starting hypothesis. Identify the original problem, the operative relation or mechanism, the conditions under which it holds, and the correspondence to the target task. Locate a condition that might fail, then propose a check with different predictions under transfer and a credible alternative. Retrieve primary evidence for uncertain technical facts when tools are available; record unverified claims when they are not.

Do not transfer a theorem's guarantee while omitting its premises, infer a biological mechanism from a naming analogy, or call an engineering metaphor an explanation. Relevant literature can enter before, during or after reasoning; do not delay a necessary fact check to preserve a prescribed order.

## Separate exploration from confirmation

Keep exploratory ideas and observations useful while stating their provenance. Before a confirmatory test, fix the main claim, data roles, competitive comparison, resource basis, principal outcome and interpretation boundary at a level appropriate to the work. New evidence can justify revision, but preserve the earlier result and explain the substantive change. Do not rewrite an observed pattern as a prior prediction.

Prefer the smallest check that changes the decision: an analytical counterexample, source comparison, an intervention check, a matched pilot or an independent interpretation. Check whether the intervention actually changed the intended mechanism. A software run that did not activate the mechanism is a failed test implementation, not a refutation of the mechanism.

## Diagnose and deepen without rescuing every idea

- Positive signal: test the strongest alternative explanation, then examine reproducibility and an informative boundary. Additional data, model capacity, selection or budget may account for apparent gains.
- Mixed or imprecise signal: retain both directions and choose new information that separates explanations. More measurements of the same unit may not resolve the uncertainty that matters.
- Ineffective intervention or measurement: repair the test if authorized; state that the hypothesis remains untested by that run.
- Effective test contradicts the predicted consequence: stop or narrow this formulation. A new premise or mechanism can justify a new candidate; renaming or retrospective metric selection cannot erase the failed version.

Do not extrapolate a finite negative result into universal impossibility, or turn a small positive example into a general law. Preserve continue/adjust/stop/inconclusive conditions in the project's existing record when needed. Execute only within the current task's data, cost and tool authorization; this reference grants no new execution authority.
